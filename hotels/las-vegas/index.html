Where should you stay in Las Vegas?

Choose the location for your repeated journeys.

For a varied first Strip visit, start with Central Strip. Put your must-see show, attraction and evening dining addresses on a map before comparing hotel entrances. A central base is a useful starting shortlist, not a guarantee that every attraction is walkable.

Choose South or North Strip for a specific anchor. If you plan substantial time at one resort or venue, being nearby can matter more than a midpoint location. Choose Downtown for repeated Fremont Street plans, with separate transport budgeted for Strip visits. A Convention Center stay should match the correct hall; an off-Strip stay should earn its place through room needs, facilities or the wider itinerary.

Before comparing quotes: choose two acceptable areas, map the actual entrances and list the trips you will repeat. Then compare the same room needs and all payable charges. A lower nightly figure alone does not establish a lower trip cost.

Once you have an area shortlist, check the property and room terms before paying. If using the partner link below, confirm that the destination actually offers the accommodation you need; the link is not a hotel-specific recommendation or a claim of available rooms.

Compare six practical Las Vegas hotel areas

South, Central and North Strip are planning labels with approximate boundaries. The official Las Vegas visitor maps help separate the Strip, Downtown and the Convention Center. Inspect the precise address rather than treating a district name as a transport promise.

South Strip: shows, stadium plans and time at the resort

Traveler fit: Travelers with a southern Strip show, venue or chosen resort as a main trip anchor. Advantages: You can organize local resort visits around that anchor instead of crossing the city every evening. Drawbacks: Central Strip, northern venues and Fremont Street still require separate journey planning. Large resort grounds make a short-looking map distance misleading.

Transport and sightseeing: Use the Mandalay Bay–Luxor–Excalibur cluster as a map reference, not a strict neighborhood boundary. MGM Grand is the Monorail's southern endpoint; that does not put every South Strip hotel beside a station. Check the actual station entrance or RTC stop and route across the road.

Arrival, budget and hotel choice: For LAS arrivals, compare a direct car pickup with the currently available bus route to your exact hotel. Do not assume airport proximity means a convenient walk. Ask about event traffic, pool-facing room noise and accessible routes through the building. Include total charges and parking rather than assuming a south-end address is cheaper.

Central Strip: a practical shortlist for a varied first visit

Traveler fit: First-time visitors balancing several Strip shows, resort sights and evening dining plans. Advantages: A suitable address around the Bellagio, Paris and Caesars Palace corridor can keep several planned visits in the same general zone. Drawbacks: Busy frontage, nightlife and event crowds may matter more than the appeal of a central map pin. Hotel entrances, pedestrian bridges and indoor routes still add walking.

Transport and sightseeing: Map each ticketed venue and the hotel entrance separately. A Monorail station name does not mean its platform is at a resort's street frontage, particularly across the Strip. Compare the station walk with a bus stop or taxi for the journey you will repeat.

Arrival, budget and hotel choice: Plan the LAS pickup and drop-off entrance in advance. Ask about room orientation, adjoining doors, nightclub or pool noise and check-in queues. Compare an acceptable central quote with another area including transport; convenience can justify a higher room quote without making it the best value for everyone.

North Strip: choose it for a northern venue or specific property

Traveler fit: Visitors whose main plans involve a northern resort, show or the SAHARA end of the corridor. Advantages: It can reduce backtracking when your important bookings are already in this part of the city. Drawbacks: The broad North Strip label covers different walking environments; the rest of the Strip and Downtown are still separate journeys. A large resort does not establish an easy pedestrian link to the next one.

Transport and sightseeing: Use the operator's station map for SAHARA and nearby rail access. Resorts on the other side of the corridor or farther from the platform may still need a substantial final walk. Check crossings, shaded breaks and a return option after your evening activity.

Arrival, budget and hotel choice: Arrange the airport route to the property entrance and compare pickup rules for later taxis or rideshare. Ask about construction, traffic, entertainment noise and the room category. Include northern hotels in a price search without assuming their charges or transport needs are lower.

Downtown / Fremont Street: make Downtown evenings the priority

Traveler fit: Visitors planning repeated Fremont Street evenings, Downtown attractions and a different base from the Strip resort corridor. Advantages: Choosing near your Downtown plans can make their evening return simpler. Drawbacks: Entertainment and street-facing rooms can conflict with early nights. A Strip-heavy itinerary adds journeys, and Downtown is not the northern end of the Monorail.

Transport and sightseeing: The Strip and Downtown are distinct areas on the official visitor map. RTC's Deuce links the corridors; check the useful stop, direction and current detours. The Monorail does not reach Fremont Street.

Arrival, budget and hotel choice: Check the current airport-to-address route rather than treating a Strip transfer as a Downtown transfer. Ask for the actual room's exposure to outdoor music, bars and loading areas, and whether a quieter position can be confirmed. Compare total prices and repeat journeys before interpreting Downtown as a budget choice.

Off-Strip: a category that needs an exact address

Traveler fit: Travelers choosing a specific property, room setup or facility, or including wider outings in their trip. Advantages: It broadens the shortlist beyond large Strip-front resorts and may offer a layout that better fits your group. Drawbacks: Off-Strip can mean just outside the resort corridor or a much more distant address. There is no single transport pattern, noise level or price advantage.

Transport and sightseeing: Map the daily destinations first, then the current RTC route or road journey. Do not rely on a promised hotel shuttle until you have its stops, operating terms and availability for your dates. A close straight-line distance is not proof of a safe or accessible walking route.

Arrival, budget and hotel choice: Compare LAS arrival and evening pickup arrangements. If considering a suburban property, including Summerlin, choose it for a trip that genuinely benefits from that location rather than as a shortcut to Strip sightseeing. Add parking or repeat rides to the quote, check nearby road noise and verify reception and room facilities.

Convention Center area: match the hotel to the correct venue and hall

Traveler fit: Attendees with repeated Las Vegas Convention Center visits, or travelers for whom a useful rail connection matters. Advantages: A suitable address can reduce daily conference transfers, with Monorail access useful for some Strip plans. Drawbacks: The LVCC campus is large, and a conference advertised as Las Vegas may use a different venue. Close to one hall does not mean close to your session entrance.

Transport and sightseeing: The Monorail has a Las Vegas Convention Center stop. Compare its location with your assigned hall and the hotel's entrance using the venue map. Verify any event transport or Loop connection separately; it is not a promise of door-to-door service from every hotel.

Arrival, budget and hotel choice: For LAS arrivals, plan luggage drop-off and reception hours if arriving before the room is ready. Check event-date quotes, cancellation terms and traffic around your hall. Compare room noise and evening dining routes, rather than assuming a business-oriented address guarantees quiet or lower prices.

Harry Reid International Airport (LAS): plan the hotel arrival

Your terminal and hotel entrance determine the route.

The Strip, Downtown and an off-Strip address need different arrival plans. Do not equate a southern Strip location with walking access from the terminal. Keep the hotel name, street address and correct drop-off entrance available offline.

The airport's official taxi and rideshare guide distinguishes terminal taxi stands from designated rideshare pickup areas. Follow current signs and app instructions for your arrival terminal, confirm the vehicle and destination, and arrange any accessible-vehicle or luggage requirements before relying on a pickup.

For a bus arrival, consult RTC's current schedules and maps for the airport route, required changes and final stop. Do not assume the Deuce picks up at your airport terminal. The Las Vegas Monorail does not serve LAS; it cannot replace the airport leg.

Use our Las Vegas airport-arrival guidance alongside the current operator instructions. Check late-arrival reception, luggage storage and what happens if you arrive before check-in. No direct transfer time or fare is promised here.

Walking, Monorail, Deuce and road transfers

Compare the complete journey, including the final walk.

Walking and summer heat

Large resorts, pedestrian bridges and routes through buildings can lengthen a walk that looks short on a map. Group nearby visits and keep water, rest breaks and a transport fallback in your plan. In summer heat, reconsider exposed routes instead of making walking a requirement of a cheaper hotel.

For reduced mobility, map step-free crossings and lifts, then ask whether the route through the property reaches your room and pickup point. A room described as accessible does not confirm every pool, venue or approach is accessible.

Las Vegas Monorail

The operator's route map shows the MGM Grand–SAHARA corridor and a Las Vegas Convention Center stop. It does not extend to Downtown or LAS, and it does not serve every resort entrance. Compare the actual station walk with your plans; use the operator's current service information before buying travel around that connection.

RTC Deuce, taxis and rideshare

RTC's Deuce route map links Strip and Downtown stops. Check direction, stop location and live alerts; traffic and detours can change the useful journey. A bus corridor does not mean every hotel door has a stop.

Recheck RTC or its journey-planning app for your dates before using a bus stop or hotel pickup as a fixed plan.

Taxis or rideshare can be useful after a late show, with bags or when the final walk is unsuitable. Verify each property's designated pickup location. For convention visits, check the exact hall and any current venue transport separately. Our Las Vegas tourist transport guide helps organize these options without replacing live service information.

Compare the total stay cost

Use comparable rooms and dates, then add the journeys.

Ask for an itemized estimated total for the stay: room charge, resort fee or other mandatory charges, taxes, and anything payable at the property. Check what has already been included in the displayed total so you do not count a fee twice. Do not assume a loyalty status, package, third-party booking or hotel location waives a resort fee.

Separate optional spending from compulsory charges. Compare parking and repeated transport where relevant, plus any facilities you would otherwise pay to use. A Downtown or off-Strip room can be worth considering, but there is no guaranteed price hierarchy across these areas.

Deposits and incidental holds: ask the property what is charged or authorized, which payment method is accepted, when it is collected and how release works. A refundable hold is different from a final stay charge, but it can reduce available funds. Confirm the release process with the property and your card issuer instead of relying on a promised timeframe.

Groups and longer stays: compare the actual beds, occupancy rules and room category. Separate rooms, a different bed setup or transport for several people can change the value calculation. Keep cancellation flexibility in that comparison rather than selecting solely by the first quoted figure.

Verify these hotel terms before booking

Confirm requirements with the actual property and your chosen booking channel.

  • Total cost: confirm mandatory charges, taxes, what is prepaid and what remains payable on arrival. Save the terms for the selected dates and room.
  • Parking: check self-parking versus valet, charges, in-and-out rights, overnight rules and whether any stated benefit applies to your booking. Free parking is not assumed.
  • Cancellation and refunds: verify the deadline, local time zone, penalties, prepayment and no-show terms. If plans change, follow the booking channel's procedure.
  • Check-in eligibility: confirm the minimum age, required identification, payment method, deposit and incidental hold for the lead guest. Requirements vary; do not infer them from another Las Vegas property.
  • Pool access: check access for the booked room, seasonal operation, closures, child rules and any separate dayclub or paid-area terms. A pool photograph does not establish access for your stay.
  • Smoking: request and confirm the required room type, then ask about smoking in public areas and the route to the room. A non-smoking room does not establish a smoke-free casino or whole building.
  • Sleep: check room position relative to bars, outdoor stages, clubs, pools, elevators and roads. Read recent property-specific noise comments and ask what can be confirmed rather than treating a request as guaranteed.
  • Room and accessibility: verify bed setup, occupancy, bathroom configuration, air conditioning and step-free access. Discuss your specific needs for showers, lifts, doors and pickup points; do not rely on a generic accessibility label.
  • Arrival: verify reception hours, late-arrival handling, luggage storage and the correct entrance. A shuttle must have confirmed routes and terms before it becomes your airport plan.

Las Vegas hotel-area FAQs

Location choices and booking checks.

What is the best area to stay in Las Vegas for a first visit?

Start by comparing Central Strip hotels if your plans span several Strip attractions and shows. South or North Strip can work better for a specific venue or resort-focused stay. Map the exact entrances and evening return routes before deciding; no area suits every trip.

Should I stay on the Strip or in Downtown Las Vegas?

The Strip suits a trip centered on its resorts and shows. Downtown suits repeated Fremont Street evenings and nearby Downtown visits. They are separate bases, so include the cost and effort of journeys between them rather than assuming both are walkable from one hotel.

Does the Las Vegas Monorail reach Downtown or the airport?

No. Its route runs between MGM Grand and SAHARA, with a Las Vegas Convention Center stop. It does not serve Downtown or Harry Reid International Airport. Check the walk from your hotel to the useful station and plan a separate airport arrival.

Are Downtown and off-Strip hotels always cheaper?

No. Compare the same dates, room category, mandatory charges, taxes, parking and expected transport costs. A lower room quote can lose its advantage if it adds repeated paid transfers. Check the actual property's terms rather than assuming a district guarantees savings.

What fees and rules should I check before booking?

Confirm the estimated total stay cost, mandatory charges, taxes, parking, deposits and incidental holds. Also verify cancellation terms, minimum check-in age, pool access, smoking policy, accessibility and the exact room category with the property before booking.

Can I visit Las Vegas without renting a car?

A trip focused on selected Strip or Downtown clusters can work with walking, RTC buses, a useful Monorail connection and taxis or rideshare. Check each route, heat exposure and luggage needs. An off-Strip base or wider outings may justify a car, but parking and rental terms still need comparison.
