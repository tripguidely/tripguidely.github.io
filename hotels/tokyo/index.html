Where to stay in Tokyo: the quick answer

Match your base to the days you will actually spend there.

For a first visit with several days in western Tokyo, shortlist Shinjuku and Shibuya. Shinjuku offers a broad transport base; Shibuya puts shopping and lively evenings close to your room. If arrival logistics or onward rail travel matter more, compare Tokyo Station. For an eastern Tokyo itinerary, consider Ueno or Asakusa rather than crossing the city each morning.

Ginza suits travelers who want shopping and dining with subway connections. Akihabara earns a place when electronics, anime or gaming shape the trip; Roppongi when art and evenings are priorities. These are traveler-fit suggestions, not a ranking that makes one neighborhood best for everyone.

The station exit matters: a hotel advertised as “near Shinjuku” can still involve a long walk from your platform. Map the actual entrance, the line you plan to use and an exit with suitable luggage or step-free access.

Seven Tokyo hotel areas to compare

Look beyond the neighborhood label to the street and station.

Shinjuku: a flexible base for western Tokyo

Traveler fit: first-time visitors combining several western districts, dining and day trips. JR services and multiple subway lines make this a useful starting point, but the station's scale rewards planning.

Advantages: shops, restaurants and rail connections support varied days. The west side has an office and high-rise character, while the east side includes busy entertainment streets around Kabukicho. Decide which setting you want to return to at night.

Drawbacks: crowds, complex exits and evening noise can outweigh a convenient station name. Shinjuku, Shinjuku-sanchome and Seibu-Shinjuku are different arrival points; check which one serves your route instead of treating them as interchangeable.

Transport and hotel choice: compare the entrance-to-platform walk for your actual line. Narita Express serves Shinjuku, subject to the train you choose; allow for the final station walk. Check room soundproofing, reception access and total quotes across both sides of the station. A quieter room position may be more valuable than proximity to nightlife.

Shibuya: shopping, contemporary culture and evenings out

Traveler fit: visitors prioritizing shopping, cafes and western Tokyo days, with lively evenings near their accommodation. The crossing and surrounding shopping streets give the area a very different feel from an office district.

Advantages: JR and subway connections help combine Shibuya with other districts. The Ginza, Hanzomon and Fukutoshin lines offer different directions, so a hotel near the line you need can reduce transfers.

Drawbacks: busy streets and station navigation can be tiring with luggage. A property farther from the busiest frontage may improve sleep but add hills or walking; inspect the route and recent room-specific noise comments.

Transport and hotel choice: Narita Express serves Shibuya on the relevant services. Compare your airport train's stop with the hotel's entrance, not just a map radius. For a three-day Tokyo trip, weigh evening convenience against the journeys to eastern sights. Compare room space and cancellation terms before paying more for the area name.

Ginza / Tokyo Station: central shopping or rail logistics

Traveler fit: travelers choosing Ginza for shopping and dining, or Tokyo Station for airport and onward rail connections. These neighboring areas solve different problems; a “Ginza/Tokyo Station” search label does not put every hotel beside the same platforms.

Advantages: Ginza has several subway access points, including Ginza and Higashi-Ginza; Yurakucho adds JR access. Around Tokyo Station, Marunouchi and Yaesu sit on opposite sides of the station, which matters for arrival and onward travel.

Drawbacks: the room quote may be harder to justify if your days and dinners are mostly in Shinjuku or Shibuya. A hotel useful for a shinkansen departure can still require a long station walk. Neither a shopping address nor an office frontage guarantees quiet.

Transport and hotel choice: Tokyo Station is a Narita Express stop; Ginza generally needs a separately planned final leg. Choose the correct station side and check the route to your next train. Compare equally suitable room categories rather than assuming all central hotels offer more space or better value.

Asakusa: temple streets and an eastern Tokyo base

Traveler fit: visitors who want Senso-ji, traditional shopping streets and the Sumida riverside close to their accommodation. It is worth considering when eastern sightseeing matters more than western nightlife.

Advantages: local walks can form a substantial part of the day. The Ginza and Toei Asakusa lines provide useful city connections, so you can balance neighborhood exploration with trips elsewhere.

Drawbacks: temple approaches attract daytime crowds, and a western-heavy itinerary adds travel. Different stations share the Asakusa name: verify the operator and exact exit rather than assuming the nearest map pin serves your airport or sightseeing route.

Transport and hotel choice: compare the hotel's route to the station you will use, including stairs and luggage access. Check current airport connections rather than assuming every train runs through to your terminal. Include Asakusa in a value search, but judge quotes for the same dates and room needs; do not assume a traditional setting means a quiet or inexpensive room.

Ueno: museums, park visits and Narita arrival planning

Traveler fit: museum-focused visitors, families wanting park time and travelers for whom the Narita arrival route is a deciding factor. Ueno Park and the Ameyoko shopping area provide contrasting daytime settings.

Advantages: JR services and the Ginza and Hibiya subway lines support wider sightseeing. Keisei Skyliner connects Narita Airport with Keisei-Ueno, making the final hotel walk worth comparing early in your search.

Drawbacks: Keisei-Ueno and JR Ueno are separate stations, and nearby Okachimachi is another distinct reference point. Busy shopping frontage, roads and rail-adjacent rooms need individual noise checks. Western evenings may involve a longer return journey.

Transport and hotel choice: compare the door-to-door route from your actual arrival station. Search Ueno and nearby addresses alongside other suitable areas for value, without assuming a fixed price advantage. For families, verify beds, occupancy and luggage space rather than treating park proximity as evidence that the room works.

Akihabara: a focused base for pop culture and electronics

Traveler fit: visitors planning substantial time around anime, gaming and electronics, or wanting an eastern-central rail base. Staying here is most useful when the neighborhood itself is part of the itinerary.

Advantages: Akihabara has JR Yamanote, Keihin-Tohoku and Sobu services, the Hibiya subway line and Tsukuba Express access. Those connections make it possible to combine specialist shopping with days elsewhere.

Drawbacks: busy retail streets may be less appealing when shopping is not your priority. Station convenience does not eliminate transfers, and proximity to tracks or a main road deserves a room-specific sleep check.

Transport and hotel choice: map airport arrival as a separate journey; do not assume a direct airport train because the area has strong city connections. Identify the operator and station exit you need. Compare similarly sized rooms with Ueno and central options, accounting for luggage storage, late check-in and the cost of reaching your evening plans.

Roppongi: art-focused days and nightlife

Traveler fit: visitors building time around art, dining and evenings out. Include it when those interests justify the location, rather than choosing it only because a hotel looks central on a city map.

Advantages: museums, shopping complexes and nightlife give you several reasons to stay locally. Roppongi Station is served by the Hibiya and Toei Oedo lines; nearby Nogizaka is a different option on the Chiyoda line.

Drawbacks: this is a subway base rather than a JR Yamanote station address. Late activity around entertainment streets can conflict with early starts or light sleep, while a quieter property can involve a less direct walk.

Transport and hotel choice: check the airport transfer and station access for the exact hotel. Favor evidence about window orientation, internal soundproofing and walking gradients. Compare the full stay cost with other suitable areas; an upscale district label does not establish room quality, price or a booking guarantee.

Airport access and station proximity

Test the whole journey, including the last walk.

Narita: compare the train with the hotel address

JR East's Narita Express information lists Tokyo, Shibuya and Shinjuku among its destinations. Check that your selected service stops where you need it. Keisei's Skyliner serves Nippori and Keisei-Ueno. A direct train can simplify arrival, but the station-to-hotel leg still matters; it is not a door-to-door transfer.

Haneda: choose the connection, not a blanket fastest option

Haneda's rail options include Keikyu and Tokyo Monorail. Use the airport's official train and monorail guide for your terminal, then compare onward connections to the hotel. Check your actual arrival date and time, including the last usable service and hotel check-in window. A daytime rail route does not establish that the same journey works after a late landing.

What “near a station” should mean for your booking

  1. Line: identify the service useful for your itinerary. JR, Tokyo Metro and Toei are separate networks; a subway-only ticket does not automatically cover a JR journey.
  2. Exit: map the walk from your platform to an appropriate exit and the actual hotel entrance. Include crossings, lifts, stairs and luggage.
  3. Two sightseeing days: test a western and an eastern journey against the days in your plan. Tokyo's attractions are spread out; local walkability does not mean the whole city is walkable from your room.
  4. Evening return: check current timetables for the journey back. If nights out drive the trip, allow for a backup return plan and its cost.

Use Tokyo Metro's official subway maps for network geography and current operator information for service and accessibility. Lift availability and hotel step-free access need separate checks. Balance budget, sleep and travel style

Compare the full stay on the same terms.

Value: include the room and daily journeys

Compare actual quotes across two or three areas that fit your itinerary. Ueno and Asakusa are useful alternatives to test against western or central options; they are not guaranteed cheaper. Include transport, breakfast you would use and separately collected charges. A saving that requires extra transfers every morning may be less attractive on a short stay.

Check whether the quoted category is a private room, shared accommodation or a capsule-style product. Look at usable floor space, bed dimensions, bathroom arrangements and luggage space. A property photo of a large suite does not establish the features of the room you select.

Families and accessibility needs

Choose the base around your days, then verify occupancy rules, children's beds, connecting-room arrangements and the exact room category. For a stroller or wheelchair, ask about the route from street to reception, lift dimensions and bathroom access. A station exit with a lift is useful only if the remaining route and hotel entrance also work.

Light sleepers and late evenings

Shinjuku, Shibuya and Roppongi can suit evening plans, but request details about windows, nearby bars and noise between rooms. Ueno, Ginza or Asakusa can also have busy frontages. Choose the room's actual exposure and recent property-specific evidence; no neighborhood label guarantees quiet.

Short stays and changing weather

For a two-day visit, give extra weight to arrival and the first booked sight. With four days, judge the base across the full itinerary. A manageable station route also helps when you swap outdoor plans for options in our rainy-day Tokyo guide.

Check the hotel before opening a booking option

Keep the address, room and terms beside your shortlist.

  • Address: map the actual building entrance and useful station exit, not just the area name.
  • Room: verify occupancy, bed setup, bathroom, smoking policy and space for your bags.
  • Sleep: check street-facing versus internal rooms, rail noise and sound transfer between rooms.
  • Access and comfort: confirm entrance steps, lift access, air conditioning and the facilities included in your room category.
  • Arrival: confirm reception hours, late check-in and luggage storage arrangements, including charges.
  • Terms: compare the full payable amount, taxes or separately collected charges, payment timing and cancellation deadline.

Tokyo hotel area FAQs

Practical answers for choosing a base.

What is the best area to stay in Tokyo for a first visit?

Shortlist Shinjuku or Shibuya if several days and evenings focus on western Tokyo. Compare Tokyo Station for onward rail travel, or Ueno and Asakusa for eastern sightseeing. The best choice depends on your itinerary, arrival route and exact hotel address.

Should I stay in Shinjuku or Shibuya?

Shinjuku suits a varied western Tokyo plan with a broad rail base; Shibuya suits shopping and lively evenings close to the hotel. Both need careful station-exit and noise checks. Compare the actual room and daily journeys rather than choosing by station name alone.

Are Ginza and Tokyo Station the same hotel area?

No. They can be compared in one central search, but their stations and arrival walks differ. Ginza emphasizes shopping and subway access; Tokyo Station emphasizes rail logistics. Verify the hotel entrance and the platforms you need before booking.

Is Ueno convenient for arrival from Narita?

Keisei Skyliner serves Keisei-Ueno from Narita Airport, so Ueno is worth comparing for that arrival. Keisei-Ueno and JR Ueno are separate stations. Check the walk to your hotel and whether the area also fits the rest of your itinerary.

Which Tokyo neighborhood is cheapest for hotels?

No neighborhood is guaranteed cheapest for every stay. Compare Ueno and Asakusa with other suitable areas for the same dates, occupancy and room requirements. Include transport and separately collected charges when judging value.

How close should my Tokyo hotel be to a station?

Use the actual entrance-to-platform route for your line, not a fixed radius. Check the correct exit, crossings, stairs, lifts and luggage walk. A hotel close to an unsuitable line may be less useful than one with a manageable walk to the service your itinerary needs.
